# Assessment and remediation

## Ten-point question rubric

- Core concept: 4
- Reasoning or example: 3
- Boundary awareness: 2
- Clear expression: 1

Score only what the question asks. Do not deduct for an unrequested tangent or harmless speech-to-text error.

After every answer report: score, what is accurate, main gap, concise correction, and either the next question or one same-concept follow-up. Scores of 0–6 or a critical misconception require remediation. Scores of 7–10 advance unless a safety-critical misunderstanding remains.

Keep the first score unchanged. Store follow-up scores separately. Do not turn unasked questions into zeros. At the end report first-attempt total, remediation results, and weak concepts.

## Remediation

Narrow the task to one distinction. If the learner confuses “an event triggered a thought” with “the event proves a conclusion”, present two short sentences and ask which makes an unsupported inference. If two attempts fail, change the example rather than repeating the same wording.

Do not force agreement with a thinker. Evaluate conceptual accuracy, reasoning, evidence boundaries, and transfer.

## Feynman passing rule

A retelling passes when it is understandable to a 12-year-old, preserves the core distinction, avoids the learner’s earlier overstatement, and succeeds in a new scenario. Minor stylistic differences do not require another cycle.

## Sensitive topics

Philosophical distress is not itself a diagnosis. Persistent symptoms or impaired functioning can justify professional assessment without proving a particular condition. Treatment claims require evidence for the actual population, intervention, comparator, outcome, and time period. Never let a philosophical framework replace urgent or professional help.
